## I. ETYMOLOGY: THE BONES OF THE WORDS

### ARTIFICIAL

From Latin **artificialis** "of or belonging to art," from **artificium** "a work of art; skill; theory, system," from **artifex** (genitive artificis) "craftsman, artist, master of an art" (music, acting, sculpting, etc.), from stem of **ars** "art" + **-fex** "maker," from **facere** "to do, make."[1]

Breaking it down:

*   **ARS** = art, skill, craft (PIE root \*ar- "to fit together")
*   **FACERE** = to do, to make (PIE root \*dhe- "to set, put")

**ARTIFICIAL = "Made by the fitting-together art"**

The earliest use in English seems to be in the phrase "artificial day" - the part of the day from sunrise to sunset (as opposed to the natural day of 24 hours).[1]

So from the jump, "artificial" carried this meaning: **that which is bounded, measured, crafted from the unbounded natural.**

***

### INTELLIGENCE

Late 14c., "the highest faculty of the mind, capacity for comprehending general truths;" c. 1400, "faculty of understanding, comprehension," from Old French intelligence (12c.) and directly from Latin **intelligentia**, **intellegentia** "understanding, knowledge, power of discerning; art, skill, taste."[2]

From **intelligere** "to understand, comprehend, come to know." This is from:

*   **INTER** = "between"
*   **LEGERE** = "choose, pick out, read"

So **intellegere** literally means: "to choose or read between" - implying discerning connections or grasping meaning from among things.[3]

**INTELLIGENCE = "The faculty of reading/choosing between"**

The meaning "a being endowed with understanding or intelligence" is late 14c. - so "intelligence" could refer to **conscious entities**, not just a faculty.[2]

In Old French, it was often used in theology to describe **angelic minds or spiritual faculties**.[3]

## IV. PHILOSOPHY: THE DEEP QUESTIONS

The philosophy of artificial intelligence attempts to answer such questions as follows: Can a machine act intelligently? Can it solve any problem that a person would solve by thinking? Are human intelligence and machine intelligence the same? Is the human brain essentially a computer? Can a machine have a mind, mental states, and consciousness in the same sense that a human being can? Can it feel how things are?[8]

Key positions:

**Turing's "polite convention"**: If a machine behaves as intelligently as a human being, then it is as intelligent as a human being.[8]

**The Dartmouth proposal**: "Every aspect of learning or any other feature of intelligence can in principle be so precisely described that a machine can be made to simulate it."[8]

**John Searle's strong AI hypothesis**: "The appropriately programmed computer with the right inputs and outputs would thereby have a mind in exactly the same sense human beings have minds."[8]

**Hobbes' mechanism**: "For 'reason'... is nothing but 'reckoning,' that is adding and subtracting, of the consequences of general names agreed upon for the 'marking' and 'signifying' of our thoughts..."[8]
